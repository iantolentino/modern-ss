Re-crop the portraits so no head is cut, and stop circling client faces

Asked to make sure faces are shown properly. Checking that properly meant building
a face detector first, because the check I had was not measuring faces at all.

The check that was wrong
  The first version used the YCbCr skin-tone test to find a face. On these
  photographs it returned a box spanning the entire frame at 11-34% coverage --
  it also fires on warm walls, timber and beige office background. Built on
  that, the check reported 79 clipped faces, none of which were clipped. It was
  replaced with OpenCV Haar cascades (frontal and profile), pooled over several
  parameter sets and filtered by shape, because one setting missed real faces and
  loosening without the filter invents them.

  Two further bugs in the tooling, both caught by reading the output rather than
  trusting it: the crop records and the face boxes were keyed differently, so the
  join tested 36 of 535 images and called the site clean on almost no evidence;
  and density was computed from naturalWidth, which Chrome already divides by the
  effective srcset density, which invented eleven upscales that were not upscales.

What the detector found
  Seven of the nineteen officers and four more portraits were shipping with the
  top of the head cut off, and the layout then removed another 3.1% in the 4:5
  boxes. neil-dane-puno had 0.000 headroom where his source had 0.135.

  The cause was frame() in strata-scan/optimise.py. It placed the skin-tone
  centroid at FACE_BIAS down the window, and the centroid is dragged downward by
  the neck, the hands and any warm background, so for a subject in a light top
  the crop followed it down and took the head with it.

  Measuring every raw source settled the rule: all 31 have the head within the
  top 15% (min 0.024, median 0.097), which is less than the margin a brow needs,
  so the window is top-anchored for this material. Two sources, Mary Ann Pineda
  and Trevor, have the head at the very top of the download, where no crop can
  create headroom; those now sit at their source's floor rather than below it.

  Two selection bugs surfaced while fixing it. Largest-detection-wins picked a
  torso over the face on team-accountant, and "largest in the upper half" still
  admitted a torso centred at 0.502 on carlo-andreu-tayag. The rule is now the
  highest plausible detection, which is what a single-subject portrait implies.

Result
  Headroom under 5%, across the 43 portraits: 11 before, 1 after. Across every
  rendered image at 1440 and 390: no face is clipped by any box, and the five
  remaining tight cases are all Trevor at his source's limit. Face centres now
  sit at 14-39% with a median of 35%, near the intended bias of 38%.

The circles
  .stmt__sig img carried border-radius: 50%, showing nine real clients through a
  round mask. A round crop is the avatar idiom, and an avatar is what you show
  when you have no photograph; we have the photographs. The corners come off a
  face for no reason but convention. Now square, with the rule border it already
  had. The only two circles left in the stylesheet are a punched hole and a
  perforation dot, which are paper, not people.

Verification
  tools/face-verify.mjs joins the browser's measured crop rectangles against the
  detected face boxes and exits non-zero on a clipped or masked face. It reports
  the tight cases rather than hiding them, since a margin is not a failure.
  tools/contact-sheet.py renders every crop for a person to look at, marking the
  detected face, because a measurement is only as good as its detector and this
  one is a heuristic. Read the contact sheet, not the exit code.

  Full sweep still green: 40 pages 200, 113 references resolve, a11y clean,
  88 images all pointed at, contrast 5.10:1 lowest, 35 of 40 pages carry
  photography with the other 5 declared. Two remaining sub-1x densities are
  source-limited: a 1024px and a 600px original with nothing larger to serve.
